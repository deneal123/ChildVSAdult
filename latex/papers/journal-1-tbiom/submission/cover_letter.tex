Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration as a regular paper. Below we disclose, proactively,
the three points on which this submission departs from the journal's default expectations.

\vspace{3mm}
\noindent\textbf{1. The dataset is not released to the general research community, and why.}
T-BIOM's Information for Authors states that where the main results rest on a dataset that is not
available to the community, this must be explained and justified. Our results rest on face images
mined from public social-media ``then/now'' posts. We do \emph{not} redistribute them, for reasons
we consider dispositive rather than convenient:

\begin{itemize}
\item The data are biometric. Under Russia's 152-FZ (as amended by 519-FZ, 2020) public posting does
      not constitute authorization for dissemination, and Art.~11 requires written consent for
      biometric data used to identify a person. Under the GDPR a face processed for unique
      identification is special-category data (Art.~9), and the ``manifestly made public'' exception
      is read narrowly. We hold no written consent, and the subjects cannot feasibly be contacted.
\item Redistributing such a corpus would create exactly the artifact the paper argues against: a
      public, non-consensual biometric database of identifiable individuals across decades of their
      lives.
\end{itemize}

\noindent What we do release instead, so the work remains verifiable and reusable:
the complete mining, curation, training and evaluation code; all configurations and random seeds;
the de-identified benchmark protocol (pair lists by salted identifier, with age gaps and split
assignment, containing no images and no recoverable identities); a datasheet for the dataset; and
all reported metrics as machine-readable files. Every number in the manuscript is reproducible from
these artifacts given re-collection under the reader's own legal basis, and the external results
(LFW, AgeDB-30, CALFW, FG-NET) are reproducible outright, since those datasets are public.
Reconstruction from a public source is additionally demonstrated in the paper by an independent
non-VK replication (Reddit).

\vspace{3mm}
\noindent\textbf{2. Human subjects and ethical review.}
The study analyses images that their subjects had already published on public community walls,
retrieved through the platform's official API. There is no interaction or intervention with any
person, no individual is identified or contacted, no system is deployed, minors are excluded as
subjects, and the released artifacts contain no raw faces, no raw posts and no recoverable
identifiers. On that basis the study was not submitted for prospective institutional review, and the
manuscript states this affirmatively together with the reasoning --- which IEEE policy accepts in
place of an approval reference (``\dots or include an explanation as to why such a review was not
conducted''). We do not resolve the counter-consideration in our own favour: the images are
biometric and the individuals remain identifiable, which is why our release policy is restrictive
rather than open, and why we have \emph{also} requested a formal determination from our university's
ethics committee (requested \textbf{[fill in the date before submitting]}). We will forward that
determination to the editor as soon as it is issued, and we will abide by it rather than by our own
reading. \emph{Consent:} the subjects cannot feasibly be contacted, so individual consent for
participation and for publication was not obtained; the manuscript explains this, as IEEE policy
likewise permits (``\dots or explain why consent was not obtained'').

\vspace{3mm}
\noindent\textbf{3. Originality and prior submissions.}
The manuscript is original, is not published elsewhere, and is not under review at any other venue.
It is not an extension of a published conference paper, so the 30\% new-material rule for
conference extensions does not apply. An earlier version was submitted to, and rejected by, another
IEEE journal; that manuscript was never published, and the present version differs substantially:
the evaluation of the LFW forgetting control was found to be defective and has been corrected (the
previously reported accuracy cost and low-FAR degradation were artifacts of an unaligned evaluation
path and do not survive correction), and a new analysis shows that the standard random-impostor
protocol understates the task -- with look-alike impostors, frozen state-of-the-art recognizers fall
below chance on the 25-year-gap subset.
